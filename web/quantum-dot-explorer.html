Dot size and band gap

measured sizing curve [7]: E0(d) = 0.41 + 1 / (0.0252 d² + 0.283 d)  (d in nm, E in eV); the diameter for a wavelength solves E0(d) = hc/λ
effective-mass approximation [5]: E = Eg + ħ²π²/(2R²) (1/me* + 1/mh*) − 1.786 e²/(4πε0ε∞R)
hyperbolic bands [18]: E = [Eg² + 2Egħ²/m* (π/R)²]½ − 1.786 e²/(4πε0ε∞R)

PbS: Eg = 0.41 eV at 300 K [6, 7], me* = mh* = 0.085 m0 [18], ε∞ = 17.2. Both models assume isotropic masses and infinite walls; PbS has an anisotropic, fourfold-degenerate L-point band edge and finite ligand barriers, so neither is used for sizing.

Surface atoms

Rock salt puts Pb and S on a simple-cubic lattice of spacing h = a/2 (a = 0.5936 nm [27]); every atom has six nearest neighbours. All lattice sites within d/2 of the centre are counted, and an atom is at the surface when fewer than six of its neighbours are inside. The count depends on where the centre sits, so it is averaged over a sphere centred on an atom and one centred in a cube of atoms. Shell model: f = 1 − (1 − a/d)³. The count is purely geometric: no facets, reconstruction or ligand coverage.

Dark current and tunnelling

ideal diode, gap constant in T [22]: Jdiff ∝ T³ e−Eg/kT (n = 1),  Jgen ∝ T1.5 e−Eg/2kT (n = 2); ratio of two dots = eΔEg/nkT; halving step = ln 2 / (d ln J/dT)
tunnelling: rate ∝ e−β d,  β = 2κ = 2(2m*φ)½/ħ for a rectangular barrier

β of the dot film is the range measured by Liu et al. [19] for PbSe dots with alkanedithiols of different lengths, 1.08–1.10 Å⁻¹ (the midpoint is used); the alkanethiol monolayer has β = 0.79 Å⁻¹ with φ = 1.39 eV [21]; the vacuum gap assumes a 4.5 eV work function. The presentation this repository supports used 0.96 Å⁻¹ (a factor 2.6 per Å, from a review); see the README, Correction. Hopping mobility also contains thermal activation, so β is an effective value.
